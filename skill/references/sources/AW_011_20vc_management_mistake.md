## Statement-type separation

### Interviewer question and premise

Harry Stebbings asks Wang to identify his largest management or leadership error and supplies his own example about managing through fear versus trust. That fear/freedom framework belongs to the interviewer, not Wang.

### Direct statements by Wang

Wang identifies the belief that company hypergrowth required team hypergrowth as his largest management error. He says Scale grew from roughly 150 people in 2020 to more than 700 by the end of 2022 and that hiring at that rate made maintaining quality and excellence impossible.

He says deterioration was subtle and became visible months later when the organization could no longer solve challenges it previously handled. He reports that headcount then remained comparatively flat while revenue grew substantially. He later summarizes the changed view as separating company growth from team growth and investing more heavily in quality and excellence.

In the hiring discussion, Wang describes personally reviewing hiring evidence, sometimes overruling team recommendations, and using accumulated observations of who succeeded or failed to calibrate hiring decisions.

### Derived interpretations — not direct claims

- The account provides a concrete error, lagging indicators, changed belief, and corrective operating policy.
- It supports monitoring delayed consequences instead of assuming an initially invisible failure is harmless.
- It does not explain how affected employees were managed or how individual underperformance was corrected.
