## Statement-type separation

### Interviewer premise

David George characterizes Scale's 2020–2021 hiring approach as a mistake made during a broader technology-industry hiring boom and asks what Wang learned. The word “mistake” and the initial historical framing are the interviewer's premise.

### Direct statements by Wang

Wang says Scale subsequently kept headcount approximately flat. He argues that adding people does not automatically improve results and that dramatically expanding a high-performing team can damage performance and culture. He describes rapid hiring as causing regression toward the mean.

Wang says outside executives should first become deeply familiar with how a company actually works, then make small changes that can be trusted and verified before receiving latitude for sweeping changes. He rejects the idea that an executive is a “magic wand” and argues that founders should not remove themselves from consequential decision loops merely because they hired experienced leaders.

### Derived interpretations — not direct claims

- Wang appears to respond to a failed scaling assumption by changing an operating constraint—flat headcount—rather than merely acknowledging the error.
- His “small steps” and “trust and verify” approach suggests staged delegation based on observed judgment.
- These are organizational lessons, not direct instructions for supervising a learner.
