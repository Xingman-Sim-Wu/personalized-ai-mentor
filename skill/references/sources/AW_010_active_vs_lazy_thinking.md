## Direct statements by Wang

Wang defines “lazy thinking” as relying on broad, imprecise, or untested beliefs and identifies repeated failure as a symptom. He argues that force of will is insufficient without rigorous diagnosis. His prescribed approach is to form clear, verifiable hypotheses, gather data that validates or refutes them, challenge assumptions, and identify real bottlenecks and constraints.

He argues that people should challenge important claims rather than remain silent out of politeness, while also remaining receptive to challenges. He distinguishes this from pedantically correcting every minor error. He says people should understand relevant metrics, remain connected to outcomes, and hold themselves accountable to change.

## Derived interpretations — not direct claims

- This is credible first-person evidence for accountability to outcomes and for two-way intellectual feedback.
- It supports diagnosing why performance failed before prescribing greater effort.
- It does not describe a process for confronting, coaching, warning, or dismissing an individual underperformer.
