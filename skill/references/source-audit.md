# Stage 1 source audit

Date: October 3, 2026
Updated after targeted gap-closing search

## Audit result

The original six-source collection had useful material but mixed source records, analytical summaries, and preliminary persona implications. Five sources are worth retaining, one is useful only with narrower evidentiary weight, and one new authored essay materially improves the corpus.

## Source decisions

### AW_001 — Keep and expand

“Hire people who give a shit” is unusually valuable first-person evidence. It supplies concrete screening questions, distinguishes attachment to a mission from general commitment to work, and favors past demonstrated behavior over interview enthusiasm. It is also intentionally provocative startup writing, so claims equating long hours with caring must not be transferred into health-sensitive study supervision without independent justification.

### AW_002 — Keep with editorial caveat

The TIME interview contains direct self-description and an operational three-level leadership model. It is the strongest current source for detail, focus, urgency, execution, direction, culture, and learning environments. TIME states that the interview was condensed and edited, so it is not a verbatim raw transcript.

### AW_003 — Keep

The Stanford clip has a publisher-provided transcript and timestamps. It is narrow but clean evidence for ambition, optimism, solving a foundational problem, and momentum under uncertainty. It should not alone support broad claims about management.

### AW_004 — Keep for narrow use

The TBPN appearance supports intensive hands-on use of emerging AI tools and the view that engineering practice is changing. It is not a general theory of learning, and its deliberately extreme advice should not be converted into a literal work schedule.

### AW_005 — Keep with scope restriction

The authored Scale post gives direct evidence of personal involvement in a high-leverage process and a stated preference for a high quality bar. Its ideological and demographic hiring claims are outside the study-mentor use case and should not become persona instructions.

### AW_006 — Keep as topic-specific evidence

The CSIS transcript is strong primary evidence for decomposing a system, finding an underserved bottleneck, valuing speed of iteration, and pairing ambition with action. These remarks concern AI industry and government; applying them to studying will later require an explicit inference label.

### AW_007 — Downgrade pending transcript review

The Accel page provides useful timestamps and publisher summaries, but the accessible page does not expose the full interview transcript. Accel was an investor in Scale, adding promotional context. Retain it for discovery and provisional topic mapping, but do not extract strong evidence cards until Wang's exact remarks are reviewed from the audio, video, or a reliable transcript.

### AW_008 — Keep

The a16z transcript directly addresses the requested 2020–2021 scaling mistake. It distinguishes the interviewer's “mistake” premise from Wang's own account: flat subsequent headcount, damage caused by rapid team expansion, regression toward the mean, protecting high-performing teams, and staged delegation through small steps and verification. Investor relationship context should remain visible.

### AW_009 — Keep

The verified YC video and Root Access transcript close several gaps with a single coherent first-person account. Wang describes receiving blunt feedback, abandoning a mistimed medical-agent idea, returning to first principles, finding the data bottleneck, building conviction against outside noise, continuously learning, selecting long-run exponentials, and optimizing agentic feedback loops using metrics and evals. Garry Tan's “first principles” label is an interviewer interpretation; Wang's supporting explanation is direct.

### AW_010 — Keep

This authored memo is the best source found for explicit accountability and reciprocal intellectual feedback. Wang calls for verifiable hypotheses, outcome data, challenges to important claims, receptiveness to challenge, connection to metrics, and accountability to change. It addresses diagnosis of failed performance at an organizational level, not management of an individual underperformer.

### AW_011 — Keep with transcript caveat

The 20VC interview offers the clearest direct admission of a management error. Wang identifies hyper-hiring as his largest leadership mistake, describes delayed degradation, and explains the subsequent separation of revenue growth from headcount growth. Because the accessible full transcript is third-party and noisy, exact quotations require recording verification. AW_008 independently corroborates the core lesson.

### AW_012 — Keep as counterevidence on pacing

“DO TOO MUCH” is direct authored evidence of intense effort, urgency, abrupt reaction, and overextension as a leadership ideal. It does not support sustainable-work boundaries. The targeted search found no comparably credible first-person Wang material advocating rest, recovery, sleep protection, or workload limits. This absence must remain explicit.

## Stop assessment

The targeted search now has credible primary material for receiving feedback, accountability to metrics, failed-direction correction, a failed scaling decision, decision-making under uncertainty, long-run orientation, resource allocation, and agentic eval loops. Additional searches began returning repeated Substack essays, derivative profiles, unrelated people named Alexander Wang, or secondary summaries.

Stage 1 therefore stops under the user's redundancy/weak-evidence condition rather than pretending every dimension is covered.

### Remaining unsupported or materially incomplete dimensions

- **Correcting individual underperformance:** no credible first-person procedure for coaching, warning, reassignment, or termination was found.
- **Sustainable work and pacing:** no credible direct evidence supporting rest, recovery, sleep, or workload limits was found. AW_012 and other authored material point toward intensity instead.
- **Personal learning mechanics:** AW_009 supports continuous improvement and fast learning, but detailed personal study methods remain thin.
- **Feedback delivery:** evidence supports challenging important claims and receiving blunt feedback, but not a detailed interpersonal method for delivering difficult feedback safely or constructively.

Do not add a source merely because it repeats a familiar description. Resume corpus collection only if a primary source materially addresses one of these gaps or contradicts the current picture.
